Founder Story

Making Technology Meaningful

The story behind Novel Insights

Long before I became an Enterprise Architect, I was already exploring the question that would eventually lead me to establish Novel Insights:

How can complex systems remain safe, resilient and meaningful when people, technology and organisations must work together under uncertainty?

My journey began at Delft University of Technology, where I studied Aerospace Engineering through my BSc, MSc and PhD. There I spent years inside one of the most complex sociotechnical systems in the world: air transportation.

Commercial aviation does not achieve safety and reliability through technology alone. It depends on continuous interaction between aircraft, information systems, human operators, operational procedures, regulations and organisations. A change in one part of this system can have consequences elsewhere — sometimes in ways nobody anticipated.

Understanding these interactions became the focus of my doctoral research.

The patterns I found there turned out not to be about aviation at all. They appear in port operations, energy networks, financial institutions and national infrastructure — anywhere many moving parts must hold together under pressure.

Complexity, resilience and human decision-making

My PhD research examined the safety and resilience of air transportation. I built simulation models of how people and systems behave under disruption — how operators coordinate, make decisions and recover when plans break down. The technique is called agent-based modelling; the same family of thinking now sits behind the digital twins that ports and infrastructure operators use to test decisions before committing to them.

I became particularly interested in emergent behaviour: outcomes created by interactions between parts of a system that cannot be understood by examining those parts separately.

This taught me a lesson that continues to shape my work: the most difficult problems rarely belong exclusively to technology, processes or human behaviour. They emerge through the relationships between them.

Over the years my research expanded into resilience, decision-making under uncertainty, multi-agent negotiation, formal verification, artificial intelligence and organisational learning — 20+ scientific publications, cited more than 500 times. My most widely cited applied research explored how convolutional neural networks and autonomous drones could support and partially automate aircraft maintenance inspection. And long before AI agents became a mainstream topic, I was researching how autonomous and human agents coordinate, negotiate and make decisions in operationally realistic and safety-critical environments.

Although the technologies varied, the underlying question stayed constant:

How can we design complex sociotechnical systems that remain reliable, adaptable and beneficial to the people and organisations that depend on them?

From aviation systems to Enterprise Architecture

When I moved from academic research into digital transformation and Enterprise Architecture, I recognised the same patterns I had studied in aviation.

Large organisations are also complex sociotechnical systems. Their strategies, capabilities, processes, information, people, governance and technologies are deeply interconnected. Yet transformation is often approached as a series of isolated technology projects.

A new platform may be technically sound but poorly connected to organisational capabilities. A process may be redesigned without considering the information required to execute it. A strategic ambition may be translated into projects without a coherent target architecture or an achievable roadmap.

The result is often more technology — but not more clarity, resilience or organisational value.

Architecture offers a way to make relationships visible, understand dependencies, anticipate systemic consequences, and align strategic intent with practical execution.

That became the foundation of Novel Insights.

Why “Novel Insights”?

The name reflects what I believe good architecture and advisory work should provide.

Organisations do not always need another extensive report or another layer of complexity. They need a useful new way of seeing their situation.

A novel insight can reveal a connection that was previously hidden. It can explain why a problem keeps returning, show how decisions affect one another, or expose where strategic ambition has become disconnected from operational reality.

My role is to help organisations find those connections and translate them into purposeful decisions, coherent architectures and achievable roadmaps.

What that looks like in practice

Today I work with governments and regulated organisations — where decisions must withstand scrutiny from auditors, regulators and the public — on complex architecture decisions, information-intensive transformation, and the governance of secure and resilient technology. My work includes complex digital transformation within Dutch public infrastructure.

My approach combines three perspectives: the analytical discipline of an aerospace engineer, the evidence-based curiosity of a researcher, and the pragmatic, integrative view of an Enterprise Architect.

In practice this usually takes one of three forms: helping an organisation choose between options that all look defensible, defining a target architecture and a realistic route towards it, or keeping a programme coherent while it is being delivered.

The deliverable matters, but it is not the objective. Its purpose is to help stakeholders understand the problem, make a responsible decision and move forward with confidence.

Two things distinguish Novel Insights from a larger firm. The person you meet is the person who does the work — senior from the first conversation to the final deliverable. And Novel Insights is vendor-independent: recommendations are not shaped by software licences, implementation quotas or vendor alliances — the advice serves the client's challenge, not a second commercial agenda.

Technology should serve a meaningful purpose

Novel Insights is built on a simple conviction: technology should not only increase efficiency — it should benefit people, organisations and society.

Security, compliance, resilience and scalability are essential, but they are not ends in themselves. Their value lies in helping organisations fulfil their purpose responsibly and sustainably.

That is what Making Technology Meaningful means to me: connecting technological possibilities with human needs, organisational capabilities and lasting value.

Let's bring clarity to your challenge

If your organisation is navigating a complex architectural or strategic challenge, I would be glad to explore it with you.

A first conversation is not about selling a predetermined solution. It is about understanding the challenge, identifying the relationships that matter, and establishing a practical next step.

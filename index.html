When to involve Novel Insights

Three recurring situations

  • A consequential decision involves competing options, unclear dependencies and trade-offs nobody has made explicit.
  • There is strategic direction, but no coherent target architecture and no realistic sequence for getting there.
  • Several programmes, platforms and suppliers are moving at once, and their decisions have stopped adding up to a coherent whole.

These situations often extend into solution and integration design, where the same dependencies have to hold at a technical level.

Coherence is not an accident. It is a design choice.

Approach

A structured approach, built for complexity

The sequence iterates. As an organisation responds, the framing and the map are revisited — an architecture that cannot be reassessed stops being useful.

01

Frame

Establish the real question, constraints, stakeholders and what a successful outcome would look like.

02

Map

Make the relationships visible: dependencies, interactions and the points where consequences propagate. Views and viewpoints are developed around the concerns of specific stakeholders, so that different parts of the organisation can reason about the same system.

03

Structure

Develop realistic options with explicit trade-offs, then define the target picture, principles, governance and roadmap.

04

Support

Remain involved as architectural decisions meet delivery reality — reviews, workshops and advice, at the pace the delivery teams actually work.

Areas of knowledge

The subjects this work involves

Information architecture and governance
Connecting services, processes, information objects, systems and ownership — including the knowledge platforms through which that information is published and maintained — so the whole chain can be seen and governed.
Integration and platform transformation
Designing how platforms, suppliers and systems fit together, and how an organisation moves between them without losing continuity.
Resilience and critical systems
Revealing how critical services depend on technology, information, people, suppliers and governance, and where continuity may fail.
Responsible AI and adaptive systems
Data foundations, lineage and ownership; monitoring for drift; and the accountability structures that keep human judgement in the loop when systems learn, adapt or automate consequential decisions.

From the founder

Dr. Soufiane Bouarfa

My doctoral research examined how complex sociotechnical systems behave under uncertainty — how outcomes emerge from the interactions between people, technology and organisations rather than from any single part. Those outcomes can be beneficial or damaging, and either way they stay invisible until the interdependencies are modelled. Large organisations work the same way: what goes wrong rarely sits inside one component. It sits in the relationships between them, and what remains implicit cannot be governed.

Novel Insights brings that perspective to governments and regulated organisations, combining research rigour with practical architecture. Vendor-independent, and senior throughout.
